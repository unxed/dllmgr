Further testing isolated a presentation difference without FastStone. A small public-API test paints a fullscreen underlay yellow, then shows an owned fullscreen popup with no class background brush. The popup paint handler validates the paint region without drawing content. Before the event queue is processed, stock Wine 11.0 shows black at the centre; after a bounded message loop it becomes white. A subsequent small corner paint leaves the centre white until a full paint restores yellow. Native Windows retains the yellow underlay at the same stages, including after the message loop. This identifies the event-processing/exposure step missing from my earlier diagnostic; it does not establish a documented preservation contract for unpainted pixels or prove that every detail of FastStone has the same cause.

Disabling ClientSideGraphics only for FastStone was also tested in a disposable prefix. It removes the WHITE phase, but replaces it with a black interruption after the image has already appeared, lasting about 123-162 ms in two observations. Restoring the setting reproduces the white phase again. I am rejecting this as a workaround because the image still flashes. Both blank colours are now checked at nine points.

The application observer was made less expensive: it reads the centre first and verifies the other eight points for a white or black candidate. Three native Windows runs collected roughly 236-242 valid samples over the observation interval, rather than the original 44, without a sampled image interruption. Wine still reproduces it. The Windows runs are at 96 DPI and 1024x768, while the Wine stand is at 192 DPI and 3072x1728, so this remains a sampled, unmatched-resolution comparison. No correct fix is established yet.
