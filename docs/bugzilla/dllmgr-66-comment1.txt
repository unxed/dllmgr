Text:

Where this shows up in practice: Adobe Photoshop CS2 (trial, Wine 11.0,
English UI). Its palettes draw small Tahoma with ExtTextOut, Tahoma height
-10, ANSI_CHARSET, no smoothing requested:

1. History palette: the name of every history state is drawn in Tahoma Bold
   10 px. After opening a file the first state reads "Open": "O" and "p"
   touch, then a gap opens before "e". Longer state names ("New Layer",
   "Merge Visible", "Duplicate Layer") get the same uneven spacing.
2. Layers palette: the locked background layer is labelled "Background" in
   simulated italic Tahoma 10 px (there is no italic face). In Wine it is
   wider than on Windows, the "B" stands apart from "ackground", and without
   smoothing the letters break up; with Wine's default smoothing of
   simulated styles it comes out grey and faint while Windows draws it
   crisp.
3. The palette labels "Opacity:", "Lock:" and "Fill:" (Tahoma 10 px
   regular) are drawn with ETO_CLIPPED into rectangles that end exactly at
   the text width Windows produces ("Opacity:" at x = 1 in a 37 px wide
   rectangle). Regular Latin is already right, but any change to Tahoma's
   glyph placement has to keep these widths, otherwise the colons are cut
   off.

The same History and Layers palettes on Windows (ClearType on) show even
spacing in all three places.
