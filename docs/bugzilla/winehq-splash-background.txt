Summary of what we found about the Photoshop CS2 splash screen under Wine
(Wine 11.0 and wine.git master, X11, 96 and 192 DPI, CS2 9.0 tryout), in
case it helps a proper fix.

1. The splash is a plain top-level window (class PSFloatC, style 0x94000200,
   ex-style 0x00000101). It is not layered and has no window region. The
   rounded card, the feather and the soft shadow are drawn by Photoshop
   itself into its own rectangular window. Photoshop's main thread does not
   pump messages while the splash is up; it paints through GetDC/ReleaseDC.

2. Photoshop composes the shadow and the antialiased edges over the existing
   contents of its own, just-shown window: it expects the window to contain
   what was on the screen under it. In Wine a new window's surface starts
   filled with 0xFFFFFF (white; older Wine versions showed black, as in the
   original report), so the margin outside the card becomes a white (or
   black) rectangle.

3. Confirmation: filling a new top-level window, right after it is shown,
   with the screen contents under it (captured from the screen DC before the
   window appears) makes the white rectangle disappear, with the card and
   shadow drawn over the real background. We did this in a version.dll proxy
   loaded into Photoshop only (WH_CALLWNDPROC hooks: capture on
   WM_SHOWWINDOW, draw on WM_WINDOWPOSCHANGED with SWP_SHOWWINDOW). Capture
   and drawing must be done in physical pixels for DPI-unaware applications
   at 192 DPI, otherwise the copy is scaled twice.

4. A snapshot alone leaves the margin frozen: anything that changes behind
   the splash later (a window opened, the main window minimized) is hidden
   by the stale copy. So we additionally cut the untouched margin out of the
   window with SetWindowRgn after Photoshop has drawn: pixels still equal to
   the snapshot were never painted by Photoshop and are pure background. The
   margin then is really see-through and live; only the few pixels of the
   soft shadow keep the background of the moment they were drawn.

5. While doing 4 we hit a Wine bug in the X shape built from a window region
   on the client-side rendering path (bug 60746): a span that starts and
   ends inside the same byte of the 1-bit mask (for example x 4..8 at 192
   DPI) is dropped, which left a fringe of 16 white or grey pixels left of
   the feather. With that line fixed the fringe is 0 pixels.

6. Screen reads: BitBlt from the screen DC over an area covered by a shown
   but not yet painted window returns white (0xFFFFFF) on Wine, i.e. Wine's
   own surface contents rather than what X shows there.

What would fix it in Wine without application-specific code, as far as we
can tell: initialize the surface of a newly shown top-level window from the
screen contents under it (what Windows without composition effectively
shows before the application paints), instead of a constant colour; a
complete solution for the shadow would need the application's drawing to be
over a live background, i.e. composition. On Windows the CS2 splash shows the
card and its soft shadow over whatever is behind it.
